The PM role

Sits where business, user experience and technology meet. Decides what gets built and why; leads through reasoning and trust, not authority.

Martin Eriksson, Mind the Product (2011)

Persona

One specific person in one specific situation: who, goal, context, constraint. “Students 18 to 25” is too broad.

Personas Make Users Memorable, NN/g

Jobs To Be Done

People hire a product to get a job done. Every job has a functional, emotional and social layer.

When I ___, I want to ___, so I can ___
Christensen et al., HBR (2016)

The milkshake study

Commuters bought morning milkshakes to fill a dull drive and stay full till noon. The real rivals were bagels and doughnuts.

HBS Working Knowledge (2011)

The Mom Test

Ask users about their past behaviour, not your idea. Compliments and “I’d use it” are not evidence.

Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test

Influence without authority

Nobody reports to a PM. You move engineers, designers and business teams with clarity and data.

Common industry practice

The breakdown

narrow, then look three ways

Review vs teardown vs breakdown

A review rates, a teardown describes, a breakdown explains why it’s built this way and proposes a fix you can measure.

Masterclass framing

Three lenses

Business: who earns from this? User: where does it hurt? Tech: what limits the fix?

Masterclass framing, built on the PM Venn

Business model

Ads want time spent, subscriptions want felt value, commission wants more orders. The model explains the screen.

Common industry practice

User journey map

Lay out the steps a user takes and mark where effort rises and mood drops.

Journey Mapping 101, NN/g

Tech dependencies

A screen shows data from many systems. One slow source (a rider’s GPS, a bank) breaks the experience and users blame the app.

Masterclass example

Why users act that way

the psychology behind friction

System 1 / System 2

Fast automatic judgement vs slow deliberate thinking. Screens are judged by System 1 before they’re read.

Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011)

Labour illusion

People value results more when they see the real work behind them. Show real progress; never fake a delay.

Buell & Norton, Management Science (2011)

Cognitive load

Every extra thing to figure out is a reason to quit, worst at the paywall.

Minimize Cognitive Load, NN/g

Fitts’s law

Bigger, closer targets get tapped more. The biggest button shows what the company wants most.

Raluca Budiu, NN/g (2022)

Nudge vs dark pattern

Would the user feel tricked if they saw how it works? A nudge helps the job; a dark pattern hijacks it.

Metrics & fixes

a fix without a metric is an opinion

North Star metric

One number that captures value to users and drives the business. Food app: orders delivered.

Amplitude, North Star Playbook

Input metrics

Levers a team can move that push the North Star: repeat order rate, search success.

Amplitude, North Star Playbook

Guardrail metric

A number that must not get worse while you push the main one. More time on a portal can just mean more searching.

Common industry practice

The fix format

change → metric → guardrail → why

Every fix answers all four, ranked by impact vs effort.
